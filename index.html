Capabilities

One shop, requirement to acceptance data

01

Test stand modernization

Replace obsolete gauges, flowmeters, controls, and instrumentation while retaining the test capability you already rely on. We reverse-engineer existing equipment, rebuild the documentation, and add modern controls and data acquisition — for the bench whose manufacturer is gone, whose drawings are missing, and whose expert retired.

02

Flow measurement

Turbine, Coriolis, and laminar-element flow sections sized to your component and media, with repeatable flow-versus-Δp behavior for production acceptance.

03

Pressure and proof test

Regulated supply, intensifiers, burst shields, and proof, leak-decay, and cycle sequences with ramp and dwell control. Relief sizing and interlocks are part of the design, not an afterthought.

04

Functional test

Pressure-switch actuation and reset points, solenoid and actuator function, RPM, temperature, LVDT position, continuity and insulation checks — the electrical half of the acceptance procedure.

05

Fixtures and manifolds

Machined aluminum and stainless manifolds, adapter blocks, AN/MS and dynamic-seal interfaces, and quick-change tooling so one bench covers a family of part numbers.

06

Controls and data acquisition

PLC or embedded control, touchscreen sequencing, recipe per part number, and CSV/PDF test records that drop into your quality system instead of a clipboard.

07

Calibration hardware

Reference orifices, calibrated restrictors, master nozzles, and transfer standards — serialized and set up for re-certification on a schedule you control.

08

Field and line support equipment

Portable pressure and nitrogen carts, hydraulic mules, and ground support test sets built to survive a ramp instead of a lab bench.

Equipment

Built around the part, not a catalog page

Most benches on the market make your component fit their plumbing. We start from the component drawing, the acceptance procedure, and the throughput you need, then design the flow path, instrumentation, controls, and fixturing around it.

  • Pressure test stands — proof, burst, cycle, and leak-decay with programmable profiles.
  • Functional test equipment — switch actuation, electrical function, position, speed, temperature.
  • Lubrication and oil rigs — heated turbine oil loops, filtration, and ΔP measurement.
  • Pneumatic and bleed-air rigs — heated air, mass-flow control, high-temperature instrumentation.
  • Fixtures and flow hardware — adapters, manifolds, orifice plates, metering restrictors, flow dividers.

Questions buyers ask first

How do I justify this internally?

Usually on repeatable acceptance time and scrapped-test risk: a bench that matches the procedure removes rework, argument over results, and the hours your engineers currently spend improvising a test setup. The study deliverable gives you the numbers to put in front of your manager.

Our CMM is vague or partly obsolete.

That's normal, and it's the part we're good at. We flag the ambiguous steps, propose a defensible interpretation, and get it agreed with your engineering authority before design release.

Can you work with the bench we already have?

Yes. Modernization is a large share of what we do — new instrumentation, controls, and data acquisition on existing equipment, including reverse-engineering the documentation that went missing.

Who owns the design and data?

You receive the complete documentation package for the equipment you paid for, so you can maintain, re-certify, and modify it without coming back to us for a drawing.

What happens after delivery?

Install support and SAT, operator training, a spares list, and a calibration and re-certification schedule. Support after that is a phone call, not a ticket queue.

Is there a minimum project size?

No. Fixtures, adapters, flow hardware, and single-function test sets are worth quoting, and they're often how a first purchase order gets written.

Documentation

Documentation built for aerospace quality systems

Test equipment is only as good as the paperwork behind it. Each build ships with a controlled document set — drawings, P&ID, electrical schematic, bill of material, calibration requirements, FAT/SAT records, and operating limits — under a document-control numbering scheme that drops into your system.

Instrumentation is calibrated before delivery, with certificates supplied for each device. Where your quality system requires it, we route calibration through an ISO/IEC 17025-accredited laboratory with NIST-traceable standards and supply the accredited certificates with the build.

Controlled drawings P&ID and schematics Calibration certificates FAT / SAT records Relief sizing analysis Operator training Spares and re-cert plan

We build ground test and support equipment. Airworthiness approval for installed parts stays with the design-approval holder; we support your certification and quality path with test data and documentation, and work to your engineering authority's requirements.

Process

Four phases, fixed deliverables

  1. Phase 1

    Requirements

    We read the component spec, CMM, and ATP, then pin down media, ranges, accuracy needs, cycle time, and safety boundaries. You get a requirements matrix and a budgetary number.

  2. Phase 2

    Design

    P&ID, flow-path sizing, relief and burst analysis, instrument selection, panel and fixture CAD, control sequence narrative. You get a design review package and a firm price.

  3. Phase 3

    Build and verify

    Fabrication, machining, plumbing, wiring, and software, then FAT against your acceptance criteria with real hardware and recorded data. You get the FAT report and calibration certificates.

  4. Phase 4

    Install and sustain

    On-site SAT, operator training, spares kit, O&M manual. You get a calibration schedule and someone who answers the phone afterward.
